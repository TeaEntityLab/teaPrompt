## Optional Worked Reference: Revisit a Model-Dependent Product Bet

**Illustrative application, not an executed experiment, new gate or mandatory workflow.** Consider a product that turns tabular notes into an editable diagram. A previous model needed repeated reminders and a custom repair step; the product also exposes a progress list and requires approval before publishing externally.

The existing lenses can separate four decisions:

1. **What is worth solving?** Business strategy asks whose repeated work the diagram replaces, what a good result means and what would kill the idea. Brief can frame the next step as a bounded prototype question rather than a fully specified product. A long-term direction is compatible with a short test.
2. **What changed with the model?** Compare the relevant previous failure cases and representative successful cases under explicit model/harness settings. Keep the existing helper as the baseline and try the simpler variant; inspect actual output and failure traces. Distinguish a missing row or broken editability from a persuasive demo. If only the new model is accessible, state the missing comparison instead of claiming an improvement.
3. **What can be removed?** The reminder/repair step is a candidate only when its originating failure is addressed. The progress list has a separate user-comprehension purpose; assess that separately. Publishing approval protects authority, not model competence, and does not disappear because generation improved. Minimality's existing Safety Floor owns the replacement decision.
4. **What informs a release decision?** Feedback from heavy users informs established workflows; include relevant novice/non-adopter evidence before generalizing. Evaluate quality, consistency, review/rework effort and end-to-end cost, not token count or prototype speed alone. Review and human acceptance decide whether the observed evidence justifies a product change.

A useful output is a source-bound decision: keep, remove, retest or reject each mechanism, with its reason and unresolved evidence. No prescribed reduction percentage, fixed interview quota, benchmark pass threshold or new model campaign is introduced here. The example does not claim that TeaPrompt improves product outcomes.
